Fart With Your Friends gameplay is built around cooperative obstacle-course movement. Two players work together to control momentum, steer through hazards, and reach checkpoints without losing control. The central challenge is not simply moving forward; it is matching both players’ inputs closely enough to avoid spikes, poison areas, walls, and awkward rebounds.

The most reliable opening strategy is to treat every section as a controlled navigation puzzle. Avoid rushing into a new platform before identifying the safest direction. If one player is pushing forward while the other is trying to turn, the shared character can drift into a hazard or bounce backward.

<Section icon={Target} title="Step-by-Step Co-op Movement Strategy">

The strongest teams use a repeatable process instead of improvising every turn. The goal is to break each obstacle into small actions: observe, align, push, stop, and verify. This method reduces accidental launches and gives both players time to react.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Read the Next Section">
    Before moving, identify the next checkpoint, the nearest hazard, and the direction of the first turn. Do not begin with a long push if the route immediately narrows.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Choose One Caller">
    Decide who gives movement commands. The caller should use short instructions such as “left,” “right,” “forward,” and “stop.” The controller should follow the latest clear command rather than reacting to every voice at once.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Use Short Bursts">
    Apply small movement inputs and check the character’s position after each burst. Short pushes make it easier to correct a bad angle before momentum carries the team into spikes.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Pause Before Turning">
    Stop near the edge of a platform or hazard and allow the character to settle. Turning while still moving can create a rebound that sends the team in the opposite direction.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Confirm the Checkpoint">
    Once the team reaches a checkpoint, pause and confirm progress. If the next attempt fails, a controlled reset can be more efficient than trying to recover from a dangerous position.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Situation | Input Pattern | Why It Helps |
|---|---|---|
| Open floor | Short forward bursts | Builds progress without excessive momentum |
| Narrow turn | Stop, align, then tap direction | Reduces the chance of clipping a spike |
| Hazard on one side | Push away from danger, then reassess | Keeps the route adjustable |
| Bad angle | Stop and reset position | Prevents a small mistake from becoming a launch |
| Near checkpoint | Slow final approach | Protects progress during the most important seconds |

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid Panic Inputs">
Repeatedly switching between left and right can create unpredictable movement. When the character starts drifting, stop inputs briefly, identify the safe side, and resume with one deliberate command.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Hazard Control and Recovery">

Hazards are most dangerous when the team treats them like ordinary movement challenges. Spikes and poison areas require spacing, timing, and restraint. A successful run often depends on refusing to take one more push when the route is already unsafe.

The gameplay footage demonstrates how quickly a team can lose control when players disagree about direction or apply too much force. That makes hazard recognition a core skill. Before entering a difficult area, identify where the character can safely stop. A safe stopping point gives the team an opportunity to realign instead of committing to a dangerous slide.

| Hazard Type | Warning Sign | Safer Technique | Common Mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spike wall | Narrow passage or exposed edge | Approach slowly and stop before turning | Holding a direction too long |
| Poison area | Colored danger zone near the route | Keep a clear buffer from the hazard | Trying to cross with uncontrolled momentum |
| Rebound edge | Wall or platform that redirects movement | Enter at a shallow angle | Launching directly into the edge |
| Blind corner | Route is hidden from one player | Let the caller scout and count down | Turning without confirming the path |
| Failed position | Character is stuck or facing backward | Use the checkpoint reset when available | Fighting the controls for too long |

<Section icon={Swords} title="Team Roles and Communication">

Good communication is the difference between coordinated movement and two players fighting the controls. The team does not need complicated callouts. It needs consistent language, predictable responsibilities, and enough silence for important commands to be heard.

Use a simple command system throughout the course:

- **“Ready”** means both players are watching the same section.
- **“Push”** means apply movement in the announced direction.
- **“Hold”** means stop all unnecessary input.
- **“Slow”** means use a lighter movement burst.
- **“Reset”** means stop trying to recover and return to the latest checkpoint if possible.

| Command | Meaning | Best Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Ready | Both players have identified the route | Before entering a new obstacle |
| Push left/right | Begin controlled lateral movement | When the caller sees a clear safe side |
| Forward | Move toward the next platform or checkpoint | On open ground |
| Hold | Stop input and allow movement to settle | Near spikes, corners, or edges |
| Reset | Abandon a poor position | When recovery is less safe than restarting |

<Section icon={Trophy} title="Progression Habits for Better Runs">

Fart With Your Friends gameplay rewards patience more than reckless speed. Teams improve fastest when they convert difficult sections into repeatable sequences. Instead of measuring progress only by reaching the end, track how consistently the team can clear each checkpoint.

A useful practice routine is to focus on one problem at a time. If the team reaches a checkpoint but repeatedly fails at the next turn, practice the approach to that turn rather than replaying the entire route mentally. Identify the entry angle, the required stop, and the amount of movement needed after the turn.

| Practice Goal | Success Standard | Adjustment if You Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Direction control | Team responds to one caller | Reduce the number of simultaneous commands |
| Momentum control | Character stops before hazards | Use shorter bursts and earlier holds |
| Turn consistency | Same route works repeatedly | Mark the stopping point before each turn |
| Checkpoint progress | Team confirms each save point | Pause briefly after reaching it |
| Recovery | Team resets without panic | Call “reset” earlier when the position is unsafe |
