Fart With Your Friends is a Roblox multiplayer raft challenge built around river movement, obstacles, and unpredictable group decisions. The phrase **Fart With Your Friends crazy chaos** fits the experience because every player can influence the raft while the route continues moving forward. The game currently presents four major activities: **Campaign**, **Raft Race**, **Endless River**, and **Daily River**. Each mode changes the reason to play, from structured progression to direct competition or endurance practice.

<Callout type="tip" title="Editor’s Tip">

<Section icon={Map} title="Step-by-Step First Run Setup">

A strong first attempt begins before the raft starts moving. New players should choose a manageable activity, identify the route ahead, and agree on simple communication rules. This approach reduces the confusion that often creates the funniest—and most avoidable—failures.

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid Early Overcorrection">
Rapidly changing direction can make a difficult section harder to read. Start with small adjustments and give the raft time to respond before changing plans again.
</Callout>

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Enter the Experience">
    Join the official Roblox experience and wait until the game area and available activities finish loading. Take a moment to identify the mode options instead of selecting the first activity without a plan.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Choose a Learning-Friendly Mode">
    Select Campaign or another cooperative river activity for the first run. Raft Race is better once the group understands how movement and obstacle timing work.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Board the Raft">
    Get into position and observe the river ahead. Look for turns, narrow sections, moving hazards, and areas where the raft may need an early direction change.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Assign Simple Commands">
    Choose one player to call the main direction while the others watch the route and provide short warnings. Use simple terms such as left, right, slow, and turn.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Make Controlled Adjustments">
    Move with intention rather than repeatedly switching directions. Small corrections are easier for the group to follow and leave more time to react to the next obstacle.
  </Step>
  <Step num="6" title="Review the Failed Sections">
    When the run ends, identify the turn or obstacle that caused the most trouble. Use that information on the next attempt instead of changing every part of the strategy.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Section icon={Users} title="Team Coordination for Chaotic River Runs">

Multiplayer success comes from role clarity. Every player does not need to issue movement commands at the same time. Instead, the group should divide attention between navigation, obstacle spotting, and quick recovery decisions.

Raft Race makes this especially important because teams can compete in formats from **1v1 through 4v4**. Larger groups have more potential input, but they also need stronger communication discipline. A short command from one agreed caller can prevent several players from reacting in opposite directions.

<Callout type="info" title="Communication Rule">
Use one primary caller, one or two route watchers, and short warnings from everyone else. This keeps information moving without turning every obstacle into a debate.
</Callout>

| Team Role | Main Responsibility | Helpful Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Primary caller | Set the raft’s main direction | Give short, early commands |
| Route watcher | Identify upcoming turns and hazards | Warn before the raft reaches the obstacle |
| Movement helper | Support the caller’s direction | Follow the agreed plan instead of improvising |
| Recovery player | Watch for mistakes and changing conditions | Call for a reset or safer line when needed |

<CardGrid cols={3}>
  <Card title="Early Calls">
    Announce turns before the raft reaches them. Early information gives the group time to respond without panic.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Shared Direction">
    Keep movement focused on one goal. Conflicting corrections are more dangerous than a slightly imperfect line.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Calm Recovery">
    If the raft drifts or loses position, pause the argument and choose the safest next adjustment.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

For cooperative activities, the best team does not necessarily make the most inputs. It recognizes when to keep the current line and when a correction is genuinely needed. For competitive races, teams should also agree on who handles calls before the match begins.

When playing with friends, decide whether the group wants a learning run or a winning run. A learning run allows more experimentation. A winning run should use familiar commands, conservative movement, and fewer unnecessary risks.

<Section icon={Trophy} title="Advanced Tips and FAQ">

Once the basic movement flow feels familiar, focus on consistency. Advanced play is not about making the most dramatic correction. It is about reading the river earlier, preserving a safe position, and keeping the team focused when the route becomes difficult.

Use these habits to improve future runs:

- Look beyond the immediate obstacle and prepare for the next turn.
- Keep commands short enough to understand during fast sections.
- Avoid changing the plan without explaining why.
- Use cooperative modes to test ideas before entering Raft Race.
- Review one failure point at a time instead of rebuilding the entire strategy.
- Treat Endless River as a practice space for endurance and patience.
- Return to Daily River when you want a recurring challenge with a clear goal.

<Callout type="success" title="Reliable Improvement">
The fastest way to improve is to repeat a difficult section with one focused adjustment, then keep the changes that produce a steadier run.
</Callout>
