<Section icon={Settings} title="Step-by-step multiplayer lobby setup">

Use the following sequence whenever your group wants to play together. The wording of buttons may differ, but the order is useful for organizing a first session.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Choose one host">
    Decide who will create the session. The host should have a stable connection and remain in the lobby while the other players join. Avoid having several players create separate sessions at the same time.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Open the multiplayer option">
    From the main menu, select the option associated with multiplayer, co-op, lobby, or playing with friends. If the game presents public and private choices, use the private or invite-only option for a known group.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Invite every friend">
    Send invitations through the available in-game menu or connected platform overlay. Ask each friend to confirm when the invitation is accepted rather than assuming that a delayed response means they joined.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Verify the party">
    Check the player list, ready indicators, or lobby display. Make sure every intended teammate is present before selecting the level or starting the session.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Start with a coordination plan">
    Agree on a simple callout system before moving. Use short commands such as “wait,” “go,” “checkpoint,” and “slow” so everyone understands the next action.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The host should avoid launching immediately after the first friend joins. A short verification pause is useful because multiplayer sessions can take different amounts of time to load. It also gives players a chance to adjust their audio, controller, or keyboard settings before the group reaches a difficult obstacle.

| Setup stage | What to confirm | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Host creation | The correct multiplayer mode is selected | Opening a solo or public session by accident |
| Invitations | Each friend accepts the current invite | Joining an older or different lobby |
| Party check | Every player appears before launch | Starting with one friend still loading |
| Audio check | Players can hear basic callouts | Discovering voice issues during a timed section |
| Level launch | Everyone is ready to move | Rushing before the group agrees on timing |

<Callout type="success" title="Reliable Host Habit">
Keep the same host for the entire session when possible. It gives the group one consistent lobby and makes reconnecting easier after a failed attempt.
</Callout>

<Section icon={MessageCircle} title="How to coordinate after joining">

Joining successfully is only the first part of a good co-op run. Shared obstacle sections often become harder when players move at different speeds or trigger actions without warning. The most effective groups use simple communication and treat checkpoints as regrouping points.

The gameplay reference highlights repeated calls to slow down, wait, and move together. Those habits are practical for new groups because they reduce accidental pushes, mistimed movement, and situations where one player reaches an obstacle while the others are still behind.

<CardGrid cols={4}>
  <Card title="Callouts">
    Use short commands such as “wait,” “go,” “left,” “slow,” and “checkpoint.” Clear words are easier to follow during loud or chaotic moments.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Pacing">
    Move as a group near hazards. The fastest player should pause instead of forcing everyone else to recover.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Recovery">
    Treat checkpoints as regrouping locations. Confirm the party is together before attempting the next section.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Patience">
    Failed attempts are normal in cooperative obstacle play. Reset the plan instead of blaming one teammate.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

For a first session, assign one person to make the timing calls. This does not mean that player controls everyone; it simply prevents four people from giving different instructions at once. Rotate the caller later if the group prefers a more relaxed style.

| Situation | Best group response | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One player reaches a hazard first | Stop and describe the next movement | Triggering the section without warning |
| A teammate falls behind | Wait at a safe area or checkpoint | Continuing until the group is separated |
| Several attempts fail | Change timing or assign a caller | Repeating the same rushed approach |
| Voice chat becomes noisy | Use short, consistent commands | Talking over every movement |
| The team reaches a checkpoint | Confirm everyone is ready | Assuming the checkpoint solved every problem |

<Callout type="warning" title="Do Not Rush the Group">
A fast solo movement may look efficient, but cooperative obstacles often require synchronized timing. Wait for the team before committing to a dangerous section.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Joining problems and connection checks">

When a friend cannot join, work through the simplest checks first. Confirm that the host is still in the lobby, the invitation belongs to the current session, and the joining player has reached the correct multiplayer screen. If the problem continues, both players should restart the invite process rather than repeatedly clicking an unresponsive prompt.

The exact cause depends on the game version, platform, network, and session privacy settings. The table below focuses on practical checks that do not require guessing at a specific error code.

| Symptom | First check | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Friend sees no invite | Confirm the host is still in the lobby | Resend one current invitation |
| Friend accepts but does not appear | Check whether the game returned to the main menu | Reopen multiplayer and join again |
| Lobby is full or incorrect | Compare the visible player list | Close the session and create a fresh one |
| Player disconnects during loading | Check connection stability and game status | Rejoin from the current host session |
| Voice chat fails | Review in-game audio settings | Use an external call temporarily |

Avoid changing several settings at once. If the host changes privacy, restarts the game, and sends multiple invitations simultaneously, it becomes difficult to identify what solved the problem. Make one change, retry the join, and then continue to the next check if needed.

<Callout type="info" title="Connection Troubleshooting">
If the party remains incomplete, rebuild the session from a clean lobby. A fresh host session is often easier to verify than an invitation chain created before everyone was ready.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Trophy} title="First-session plan and FAQ">

A short preparation routine makes the first multiplayer attempt smoother. Before launching a level, confirm the host, party list, communication method, and first regrouping point. The goal is not to eliminate every mistake; it is to make mistakes easy to understand and recover from.

Use this plan for a new group:

1. Select one host before anyone launches multiplayer.
2. Invite friends and wait for visible confirmation.
3. Test one simple movement or callout.
4. Agree that checkpoints are regrouping points.
5. Start the level only after the party is complete.

| First-session goal | Success signal | Useful adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Join the same lobby | Every intended player is visible | Rebuild the lobby if the list is incomplete |
| Establish communication | Everyone understands basic callouts | Use shorter commands |
| Reach the first checkpoint | The group arrives together | Slow the lead player |
| Recover from failure | Players regroup without restarting unnecessarily | Change timing instead of repeating blindly |
| Finish the session cleanly | The host remains available for the next round | Leave together before creating another lobby |

<Callout type="tip" title="Best Beginner Rule">
For the first run, prioritize staying together over speed. Once the group understands the level rhythm, faster attempts become easier to coordinate.
</Callout>
