Fart With Your Friends includes four publicly listed activities: Campaign, Raft Race, Endless River, and Daily River. Each one supports a different remote-play goal. Campaign is a practical starting point for new groups, while Raft Race is designed for direct team competition.

The exact experience of a mode can depend on the current game session and available content. Use the comparison below as a planning guide rather than assuming every activity has identical objectives or player requirements.

| Mode | Main focus | Good for remote groups | Recommended approach |

<Callout type="warning" title="Mode Selection Warning">

### Which Mode Should You Play First?

Choose **Campaign** when the group includes first-time players. It provides a structured way to understand the basic river flow without immediately adding another team as a competitive threat.

Choose **Raft Race** when everyone understands the movement pattern and wants a direct challenge. The available team structures include 1v1, 2v2, 3v3, and 4v4, so organize the lobby before the match begins.

Choose **Endless River** for repeated practice. This mode gives the team a chance to work on consistency, communication, and reactions across longer navigation attempts.

Choose **Daily River** when the group wants a recurring objective. Compare decisions after each attempt, especially around turns and difficult obstacle sections.

| Group situation | Best first choice | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| New players | **Campaign** | Introduces the basic flow in a structured format |
| Competitive friends | **Raft Race** | Provides team-versus-team play |
| Practice-focused group | **Endless River** | Encourages repeated navigation and endurance |
| Returning players | **Daily River** | Creates a recurring challenge to revisit |

<Section icon={Shield} title="Remote Play Tips for Better Raft Control">

Remote play adds a communication challenge because players are not sharing the same physical space. A useful team plan reduces that problem. Before the raft begins moving, decide who watches the route, who calls direction changes, and how the group will respond when the river becomes difficult.

Use small adjustments instead of rapidly changing direction. Looking ahead gives the team more time to react, while last-second commands can create conflicting movement. The goal is a shared response, not individual players trying to correct the raft separately.

<CardGrid cols={4}>
  <Card title="Look Ahead">
    Watch the river before the raft reaches a turn. Early information gives the caller more time to communicate.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Use Short Calls">
    Simple directions such as left, right, slow, and turn are easier to understand during busy sections.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Avoid Conflicts">
    Let the assigned caller lead movement decisions unless the team needs to change the plan.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Review Mistakes">
    Identify one problem area after each run and adjust the next attempt around that issue.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

| Common mistake | Why it causes trouble | Better response |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone gives directions | Conflicting calls delay the team | Appoint one primary caller |
| Turning at the last moment | The raft may not respond in time | Call the adjustment earlier |
| Repeating large corrections | Excessive movement can reduce control | Use smaller, deliberate inputs |
| Ignoring the route ahead | The team reacts after the hazard appears | Assign players to watch upcoming sections |
| Changing plans constantly | Players lose track of the current direction | Keep one plan until new information requires a change |

<Callout type="info" title="Team Strategy">
The strongest remote groups treat communication as part of raft control. Decide the plan before the run, make measured adjustments, and review one clear improvement after each attempt.
</Callout>

### Improving Raft Race Performance

Raft Race is more demanding because the team must navigate the river while competing against another group. Begin with even team sizes whenever possible. A balanced setup keeps the focus on coordination rather than an uneven lobby.

Use a short pre-race plan:

- Decide who calls the first turn.
- Confirm the direction words everyone understands.
- Identify whether the team prefers cautious or aggressive movement.
- Agree that the caller can change the plan when the route changes.
- Review the first major mistake after the race.

Do not assume that faster commands automatically create better movement. Good timing matters more than constant chatter. A calm caller who communicates early can help the team respond more consistently.
