<Section icon={Target} title="Raft Coordination Tips for Friends">

Multiplayer success depends less on constant movement and more on synchronized decisions. When every player reacts independently, the raft can receive conflicting input at the same moment. A simple command structure gives the group one shared plan.

Before the run, select a caller and define the meaning of common commands. The caller should watch the route ahead, announce the next direction early, and avoid changing instructions unless the obstacle requires it.

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid Conflicting Inputs">
Do not let every player make a different emergency call. One clear direction is easier to follow than several late commands, especially near turns and obstacles.
</Callout>

| Team Role | Main Responsibility | Useful Habit |
|---|---|---|
| Caller | Announces turns and obstacle responses | Speak early and use short commands |
| Navigator | Watches the river ahead | Identify turns before the raft reaches them |
| Support Player | Follows the agreed movement plan | Avoid sudden opposite inputs |
| Team Coordinator | Manages mode and regrouping | Confirm everyone is ready before starting |

Use the following communication pattern:

- **Look ahead:** Identify the next turn, narrow passage, or difficult section.
- **Call early:** Announce the intended direction before the raft reaches the obstacle.
- **Confirm briefly:** Teammates acknowledge the instruction without creating a long conversation.
- **Adjust carefully:** Make small changes when the raft begins responding.
- **Review after failure:** Identify whether the problem was timing, direction, or unclear communication.

A good caller does not need to speak constantly. The goal is to provide useful information at the right time. Excessive commands can make players hesitate, while late commands leave little time to react.

<CardGrid cols={3}>
  <Card title="Clear Calls">
    Use simple words such as **left**, **right**, **slow**, and **turn**. Short commands reduce reaction time.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Early Reading">
    Watch the route ahead instead of waiting for the obstacle to appear directly in front of the raft.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Small Adjustments">

  </Card>
</CardGrid>

</Callout>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Competitive Raft Race Strategy">

Raft Race is the main competitive focus for teams that want to test their multiplayer coordination. Because the game supports formats from 1v1 through 4v4, team size can change how much communication is required. Larger groups have more players available, but they also need clearer role assignments.

Before entering a race, decide whether the priority is speed, safe navigation, or learning the route. New teams should value a clean run over aggressive movement. A controlled approach makes it easier to identify which sections need more practice.

| Team Size | Strength | Risk | Recommended Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1v1 | Simple communication | Limited room for recovery | Use direct, frequent coordination |
| 2v2 | Balanced teamwork | Players may issue opposite calls | Assign one caller per team |
| 3v3 | More reaction options | More voices can create confusion | Use one primary caller |
| 4v4 | Largest team format | Highest need for organization | Set roles before the race starts |

<Callout type="tip" title="Race Preparation">
For a first Raft Race, use even teams and agree on the caller before entering the match. This gives both sides a clear communication structure.
</Callout>

Recommended competitive habits include:

- **Choose even teams:** Balanced team sizes make the comparison easier and keep the match organized.
- **Keep one primary caller:** Additional players can report hazards, but one person should decide the next movement.
- **Avoid panic reversals:** If the raft begins turning, allow the team’s adjustment time before issuing another command.
- **Use practice runs:** Campaign, Endless River, or Daily River can help players develop shared timing.
- **Review the result:** After the race, discuss one successful decision and one section that caused trouble.

Do not assume that a specific animal, raft, or cosmetic provides a confirmed competitive advantage. The available official information identifies animal and raft content, including Penguin, Fox, Cat, Candy Cane, Toxic Slime, and Jack-o’-Lantern additions, but it does not establish a universal strength ranking for multiplayer performance.

</Section>
