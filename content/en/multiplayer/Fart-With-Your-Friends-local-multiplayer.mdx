<Section icon={Shield} title="Teamwork tips for river runs">

Multiplayer success depends less on constant movement and more on consistent decisions. Players should watch the route ahead, communicate before turns, and avoid making rapid direction changes without a shared plan.

A useful team structure has one primary caller and several players focused on observing the river. The caller announces the next action, while teammates report obstacles or confirm whether the raft is responding. This keeps communication short without preventing the group from reacting to new hazards.

| Team role | Responsibility | Useful callouts |
|---|---|---|
| Primary caller | Announces the intended direction | “Left,” “right,” “straight” |
| Route watcher | Spots upcoming obstacles | “Obstacle ahead,” “tight turn” |
| Movement helper | Supports the agreed adjustment | “Slow,” “hold,” “turn now” |
| Team lead | Chooses when to change plans | “Reset,” “try the other side” |

Follow these principles during a run:

- **Look ahead before turning.** Early decisions give the raft more time to respond.
- **Use one shared direction.** Conflicting inputs can make a difficult section harder to manage.
- **Keep commands brief.** Short words are easier to hear and remember during a fast section.
- **Review failed turns.** After a run, identify the first obstacle that caused the group to lose control.
- **Change one variable at a time.** Adjust timing, direction, or communication rather than changing everything at once.
