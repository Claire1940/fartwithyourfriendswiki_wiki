<Section icon={Compass} title="Step-by-Step Invite and Join Process">

Use this process whenever you want to play with friends. It works for casual cooperative runs and provides a clear setup for Raft Race teams.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Confirm the Group">
    Choose the players who will join the session and make sure everyone is online or ready to join Roblox. For competitive play, decide the teams before launching the activity.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Open the Official Experience">
    Start from the official Fart With Your Friends Roblox game page. Verify the title before joining so the group does not enter an unrelated or similarly named experience.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Use Roblox Join or Party Features">
    Invite friends through the Roblox features currently available to your account, or have friends join the session leader directly when the game’s privacy and join settings allow it.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Verify the Same Server">
    Wait until every player has loaded into the same experience. Ask friends to confirm that they can see the same lobby, mode area, or other players before moving forward.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Select the Activity">
    Choose Campaign, Raft Race, Endless River, or Daily River. Use a cooperative activity for practice, then switch to Raft Race when the group is comfortable with the controls.
  </Step>
  <Step num="6" title="Start With One Caller">
    Select one player to call directions such as left, right, slow, or turn. A shared plan is easier to follow than several players giving different instructions.
  </Step>
</Steps>

For a first session, Campaign or another practice-focused activity may be easier than immediately entering a competitive race. Players can learn how the raft responds, observe the river route, and agree on short commands before competing against another team.

<Section icon={Shield} title="Multiplayer Communication and Raft Tips">

Inviting friends is only the first part of a successful run. River sections can include turns, hazards, and changing movement demands, so teams should agree on communication before the raft starts moving.

Use short instructions instead of long explanations during an active section. “Left,” “right,” “hold,” and “turn” are easier to understand quickly than a sentence describing the entire route. One player can lead the callouts while the rest focus on following the agreed direction.
