<Section icon={Target} title="Remote Play Teamwork and Raft Control Tips">

Remote play succeeds through consistency rather than constant movement. Players should watch the route ahead, make deliberate adjustments, and avoid changing direction repeatedly without a shared reason. A single coordinated decision is easier for the team to follow than several independent reactions.

Use one player as the primary caller during difficult sections. Other players can provide warnings about upcoming obstacles, but the group should agree on which instruction takes priority. This approach is especially useful in Raft Race, where hesitation can affect the entire team.

| Team Role | Responsibility | Useful Communication |
|---|---|---|
| Caller | Announces the main direction or turn | “Left,” “right,” “hold,” “turn” |
| Navigator | Watches the route ahead | Warns about obstacles and difficult sections |
| Support Player | Reinforces the agreed movement | Repeats the current call briefly |
| Reviewer | Identifies mistakes after the run | Notes turns or sections that need practice |

<Callout type="success" title="Coordination Rule">
Use short, consistent commands and make small adjustments. The goal is not to issue more instructions; it is to make the important instruction easy to understand.
</Callout>

### How to Handle Difficult River Sections

Look ahead before making a turn. Waiting until the raft is already beside an obstacle can leave less time to react. When the route becomes difficult, prioritize the next safe direction instead of trying to correct every movement at once.

After a failed attempt, avoid changing the entire strategy immediately. Identify the specific problem:

- Did the group react too late?
- Were multiple players calling different directions?
- Did the team change direction too often?
- Did everyone understand the same objective?
- Was the chosen mode too demanding for the current group?

This review process makes the next attempt more focused. It also helps new players improve without treating one failed run as a complete strategy failure.

### Remote Play Advice for Different Group Sizes

| Group Situation | Recommended Plan | Common Risk |
|---|---|---|
| One player practicing | Use a manageable activity and focus on route awareness | Overcorrecting without team feedback |
| Small cooperative group | Select one caller and one navigator | Several players issuing different commands |
| Full competitive teams | Confirm team assignments before starting | Uneven organization or unclear roles |
| Mixed-experience group | Let experienced players call difficult sections | New players reacting independently |
