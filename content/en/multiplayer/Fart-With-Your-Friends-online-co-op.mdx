<Section icon={Map} title="Best Modes for Playing with Friends">

Each mode supports a different type of multiplayer session. Campaign is a practical starting point because it gives new players a structured way to understand the river and raft movement. Raft Race is better for groups that want direct competition, while Endless River emphasizes continued navigation. Daily River provides a recurring challenge for players who want a fresh objective during a session.

| Mode | Main Goal | Multiplayer Fit | Recommended Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Campaign | Progress through structured river challenges | Cooperative learning | First runs and steady progression |
| Raft Race | Compete against another team | 1v1, 2v2, 3v3, or 4v4 | Organized competitive groups |
| Endless River | Navigate for as long as possible | Cooperative or solo-style practice | Endurance and control practice |
| Daily River | Complete the current daily challenge | Cooperative or solo-style challenge | Repeat visits and group goals |

### Campaign

Campaign is the most approachable choice when the group is unfamiliar with the game. Players can concentrate on understanding the raft’s response, reading the river, and learning how much time is needed to adjust direction. Use this mode to establish communication habits before moving into a competitive race.

### Raft Race

Raft Race changes the focus from completion to team performance. The available formats run from 1v1 through 4v4, so group size directly affects how the session is organized. Even teams are easier to compare, but communication remains more important than simply filling every available position.

### Endless River

Endless River is useful for testing consistency. Instead of focusing only on one finish point, the group can observe how well it handles repeated turns and changing river sections. Treat each attempt as practice: identify where the raft becomes difficult to control, then adjust the team’s callouts.

### Daily River

Daily River gives players a recurring challenge to complete with friends or alone. Because the route objective can become a shared session goal, it works well for groups that want a shorter activity without committing to a longer practice plan.

<Callout type="warning" title="Choose the Right Difficulty">
Do not start with Raft Race if the entire group is still learning movement. A cooperative run gives players time to understand raft control without adding team-versus-team pressure.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Co-Op Raft Control and Team Tips">

Successful multiplayer runs depend on anticipation rather than constant movement. Players should watch the route ahead, communicate early, and avoid repeated direction changes. Small adjustments give the raft more time to respond and make it easier for the team to correct a mistake.

Use one communication role for major decisions. A caller can announce the intended direction, while other players watch for hazards and confirm what is coming next. This structure does not require one player to make every decision, but it prevents the group from reacting in several different directions.

| Situation | Risk | Recommended Response |
|---|---|---|
| Tight turn ahead | Late movement sends the raft wide | Call the direction before reaching the turn |
| Conflicting commands | Players make opposing adjustments | Follow the designated caller |
| Difficult river section | The team reacts without a plan | Slow the callout pace and watch ahead |
| Failed attempt | The same mistake repeats | Review the exact section before restarting |
| Competitive race | Pressure causes rushed decisions | Keep commands short and consistent |

### Practical Callout System

A useful team vocabulary can be limited to a few words:

- **Left** or **right**: the intended direction.
- **Turn**: a major change is approaching.
- **Slow**: avoid unnecessary repeated adjustments.
- **Hold**: maintain the current direction.
- **Reset**: stop reacting to old information and focus on the next section.

This system is intentionally simple. The goal is not to create complicated roles but to reduce confusion when the river becomes harder to read.
