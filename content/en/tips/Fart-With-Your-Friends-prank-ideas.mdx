Choose one friend to act as an expert who has never seen the selected route. They must give advice based only on what the team reports. This works especially well in **Endless River**, where players can focus on survival and communication instead of a single race result.

Keep the joke transparent after the first mistake. A prank should test how the group communicates, not pressure one player into repeated failures.

For a safe version of a fake emergency drill, tell the team that one person will call out an imaginary crisis such as “storm protocol” or “ghost turn.” The group then performs a pre-agreed action, such as pausing verbal chatter for a few seconds or switching captains.

Do not claim that the game has a hidden emergency mechanic. The humor comes from the roleplay and coordinated response. Keep the instruction obvious enough that players do not confuse it with an official game announcement.

### Private Group Versus Public Session

A private friend group is the safest place to test a new prank. Everyone can understand the rules, choose roles, and stop the activity without affecting uninvolved players. Public sessions require much more restraint because other players may be focused on progression, practice, or a serious race.

Use this priority order:

- **First choice:** a group of friends who agreed to the joke.
- **Second choice:** a cooperative session where all players are already chatting.
- **Last choice:** a public competitive match with unfamiliar players.

If you enter a public match, keep the joke limited to your own team’s communication and appearance. Do not fake moderator messages, impersonate HSF Studios, or tell other players that they have earned rewards.
