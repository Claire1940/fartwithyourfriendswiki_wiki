<Section icon={Target} title="Fart With Your Friends challenges: Core Rules">

The best Fart With Your Friends challenges are easy to understand, quick to restart, and flexible enough for different group sizes. Start by choosing a single objective, then define how players earn points and how a round ends. Clear rules prevent arguments and give everyone room to focus on timing and humor.

Keep the first session relaxed. A challenge should create funny moments rather than punish players for experimenting. If the game includes different tools, effects, sounds, or interaction options, allow a short practice round before scoring begins.

| Rule Element | Recommended Approach | Why It Helps |
|---|---|---|
| Round length | 3–8 minutes | Keeps energy high and limits downtime |
| Win condition | Highest score or most successful attempts | Makes the objective easy to track |
| Player order | Rotate clockwise each round | Gives everyone equal attention |
| Tie breaker | One sudden-death attempt | Resolves ties without restarting the session |
| Reset rule | Restart after a clear mistake | Prevents confusion over disputed rounds |

<Callout type="tip" title="Editor Tip">
Write the challenge rules in one sentence before playing. If the sentence needs several exceptions, simplify the format.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Users} title="Challenge Formats for Different Groups">

Group size changes the ideal format. Two players can compete directly, while larger parties usually need rotating turns or team scoring. The goal is to avoid long waits and make every round produce a clear highlight.

For four or more players, use a leaderboard with small point values. A simple system such as three points for first place, two for second, and one for participation keeps the session competitive without making one early mistake impossible to overcome.

| Group Size | Best Format | Suggested Scoring |
|---|---|---|
| 2 players | Head-to-head rounds | 1 point per round win |
| 3 players | Free-for-all | 3, 2, and 1 points |
| 4 players | Rotating solo turns | 2 points for success, 1 for style |
| 5–8 players | Teams or bracket | Team points plus optional bonus |
| 9 or more | Short tournament | Qualifiers, semifinals, and a final |

Use team formats when some players are new. Pairing experienced and unfamiliar players creates a more welcoming session and reduces the pressure on anyone learning the controls or interaction rules.

<Callout type="success" title="Fair Play">
Give every player the same number of attempts. If a round depends on a random effect, award a small participation point so one unlucky result does not decide the entire session.
</Callout>

A good rotation system can follow this pattern:

- Round one uses solo attempts.
- Round two pairs players into temporary teams.
- Round three returns to individual scoring.
- The final round uses the group’s favorite format.

This structure creates variety without requiring a complicated tournament bracket.

<Section icon={Sparkles} title="Step-by-Step Challenge Setup">

Use the following setup whenever you want to create a new challenge quickly. It works for casual sessions, community events, and repeat play with the same friend group.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Pick the Theme">
    Choose one clear theme, such as timing, surprise, accuracy, reactions, or creative combinations. Avoid combining several themes in the opening round.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Define the Objective">
    State exactly what counts as success. Examples include landing the best-timed action, earning the most votes, or completing a sequence without a mistake.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Set the Limits">
    Decide the round duration, attempt count, player order, and any actions that are not allowed. Limits make the challenge feel intentional.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Run a Practice Round">
    Let everyone test the format once without recording points. Use this round to resolve unclear rules and confirm that the challenge is comfortable for the group.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Record Results">
    Track scores after every round. Announce the current leader, but keep the final result open until the last challenge is complete.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Setup Question | Fast Answer |
|---|---|
| How many rounds? | Three for casual play, five for a longer session |
| How many attempts? | One to three per player |
| Who judges style? | The group, with self-voting disabled |
| What if rules are unclear? | Pause scoring and agree on a correction |
| What if someone disconnects? | Recreate the round when practical |

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid Rule Drift">
Do not change the scoring system halfway through a serious match. If the format is not working, finish the current round, explain the change, and reset the leaderboard.
</Callout>

After the first session, save the formats that created the most laughter and the fewest pauses. Those become your group’s regular challenge rotation.

</Section>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Scoring, Boundaries, and Replay Value">

A strong challenge needs more than a funny premise. Players should know how points are earned, what happens after a failed attempt, and whether audience reactions affect the result. Consistency matters more than complex scoring.

For comedy-based rounds, use a combination of objective and social points. Objective points reward timing or completion, while style points recognize originality. Keep bonus points limited so the group’s favorite moment does not overwhelm every other result.

| Scoring Style | Best Use | Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objective score | Timing or accuracy | Easy to verify | May feel less creative |
| Group vote | Comedy or style | Encourages memorable plays | Can favor louder players |
| Hybrid score | Mixed challenges | Balances skill and humor | Requires clear categories |
| Team score | Large groups | Reduces waiting time | Individual impact is smaller |

Before starting, agree on basic boundaries:

- Do not use a player’s real name or personal information as a challenge target.
- Avoid recording or sharing voice clips without everyone’s permission.
- Keep surprise effects within the comfort level of the group.
- Allow a player to skip a round without losing access to future turns.
- Use a neutral host to settle unclear scoring decisions.

<Callout type="info" title="Replay Tip">
Change only one variable when replaying a successful challenge. Increase the time pressure, reduce attempts, or add a new theme, but do not rebuild every rule at once.
</Callout>
